Email

Email scams

Phishing, fake invoices, account verification requests, impersonation and offers too good to be true.

  • Sender domain isn't the company's real one
  • “Final notice” pressure with a same-day deadline
  • Attachment ends in .exe, not .pdf

Phone call scams

Websites

Fake websites

Look-alike logins, fraudulent shops and forms that harvest your details.

  • Extra words like “secure-login” in the domain
  • You arrived from a link, not by typing the address
  • A padlock only means encrypted, not trustworthy

Social engineering

Social engineering and impersonation

Job offers, romance and investment schemes, and fake profiles that build trust before they ask for anything.

  • Pay far above market for no experience
  • A real employer never charges you to start
  • “Mutual connections” are easy to fake

Why BaitGuard

Quizzes teach facts.
Scams target feelings.

Most people already know “don't click suspicious links”. Scams work because, in the moment, the link doesn't feel suspicious. BaitGuard trains that moment: you feel the pressure, make the call, then see exactly how it was engineered.

  • 01

    Real experiences, not passive quizzes

    You act inside the scenario, and it reacts to what you do.

  • 02

    Practise with nothing at stake

    No real money, accounts or personal information are ever involved.

  • 03

    Understand the psychology

    Learn the levers scammers pull, so new tricks still look familiar.

  • 04

    Sharpen your instincts

    Get better at noticing when something is off, before you act.

  • 05

    Confidence through repetition

    Return to scenarios and see your judgement improve.
